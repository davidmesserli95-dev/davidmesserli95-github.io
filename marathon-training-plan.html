Sub-3:30 Marathon Plan

Race day: Sunday, May 16, 2027 · 27 weeks from November 9, 2026

Three easy runs a week through January 3 build your aerobic base. From January 4 you move to four runs a week with workouts, and from February 8 to five, peaking at 48 miles (77 km) in April. Seven races along the way double as tune-ups, and the Horsetooth Half on April 18 is the final checkpoint. Sub-3:30 means averaging 8:00 per mile (4:58 per km).

The weekly pattern

  • Nov 9 – Jan 3, three runs: two easy weekday runs (one with strides) and a weekend long run. No hard workouts, just aerobic base.
  • Jan 4 – Feb 7, four runs: Tue easy, Thu workout (tempo, intervals or marathon pace), Fri easy with strides, Sun long.
  • From Feb 8, five runs: Tue easy, Wed workout, Thu easy, Fri easy with strides, Sun long. Mondays and Saturdays are rest days.
  • Hard days: the workout and the long run. In weeks with marathon-pace miles in the long run, the midweek workout is lighter.
  • Spacing: never run hard two days in a row, and leave at least two days between the workout and the long run.

Grow into these. Workouts start in January, so run them at the slower end of each range at first, or up to 20 seconds per mile (12 per km) slower if they feel like a fight. Easy runs stay easy no matter what.

The phases

  1. Base, weeks 1–8 (Nov–Dec): easy aerobic miles. The long run grows from 8 to 13 miles (13 to 21 km) and the week from 16 to 24 (26 to 39 km).
  2. Build, weeks 9–17 (Jan–early Mar): a fourth run day and weekly workouts begin, a fifth day arrives Feb 8, and long runs on hilly routes reach 17 miles (27 km).
  3. Peak, weeks 18–24 (Mar–Apr): two 20-milers, marathon-pace finishes on tired legs, the Horsetooth Half and up to 48 miles (77 km) a week. The last big run, 18 miles (29 km) with 8 (13 km) at MP, comes three weeks out.
  4. Taper, weeks 25–27 (late Apr–May): volume drops so you arrive fresh.

Every fourth week or so is a lighter cutback week, timed to land on race weekends so you race on fresher legs. Don't skip them; that's when your body absorbs the training.

How to run each workout

  • Easy: 9:00–9:45 per mile (5:36–6:04 per km). If you can't talk in full sentences, slow down.
  • Strides: 4 × 20-second smooth accelerations to fast, not all-out, with a full walk back between.
  • Tempo: warm up 1 mile, run the tempo miles at 7:20–7:35 per mile (4:33–4:43 per km), cool down. For repeats (3 × 2 mi), jog 3 minutes between.
  • Intervals: warm up 1–2 miles, run each rep at 6:55–7:10 per mile (4:18–4:27 per km), jog half the rep's distance between, cool down.
  • Marathon pace (MP): warm up 1 mile, then hold 7:55–8:00 per mile (4:55–4:58 per km) evenly. These teach your body race rhythm and fueling.
  • Long run with MP finish: run easy, then shift to MP for the last miles without straining. This is the most race-specific session in the plan, so wear race shoes and practice race fueling.
  • Long run, rolling hills: pick a hilly route with real downhills (the foothills are ideal) to prepare for Horsetooth's climb and descents. Run by effort, not pace.
  • Races: race them hard; they replace the week's workout. Tap a race week for the warm-up and cool-down plan.

If you miss a run: skip it and carry on with the plan. Never squeeze two hard runs into the same week to catch up.

Making it work

Cross-training and strength work fill the days you don't run.

  • Cross-train 1–2 days a week: 30–60 minutes of easy cycling, elliptical, rowing or swimming builds aerobic fitness without the pounding.
  • Strength twice a week: 20 minutes of calf raises, single-leg squats, lunges, glute bridges and planks, after an easy run or on a rest day. This is cheap insurance against injury on long runs.
  • Keep easy runs easy: they build the aerobic base, so most of your miles should feel relaxed, even when the goal pace feels far off.
  • Fuel the long runs: from 14 miles (23 km) on, take a gel or chews every 30–40 minutes. Practice with what the race will hand out.
  • Rest after long runs: take the day after off or keep it very easy.
  • Shoes: replace them every 300–500 miles (500–800 km). Start a fresh pair by late March so they're broken in by race day.
  • Back off for pain: soreness that fades as you warm up is normal. Pain that gets worse or changes your stride means stop and rest 2–3 days; see a doctor or physio if it lasts.
